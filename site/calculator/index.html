Where the cheapest option changes

A single price says who wins at one volume. This says the volume at which the answer changes — the number that decides whether a migration is worth doing. The scan runs the same formula across every egress level up to 1,000 TB a month and reports where the leader changes hands.

At 1 TB stored, IDrive e2 is the cheapest choice.

  • From 3 TB/month of egress onward, Wasabi is the cheapest option ($7.99/month).

Break-even points are rounded to the nearest TB. They move with the storage volume: a provider that charges less per TB keeps the lead longer before metered egress overtakes a flat rate.

What this calculator does not cover

  • Flat bundles that cannot be scaled linearly — DigitalOcean's and UpCloud's plans are priced per 250 GB block, so a per-TB rate would be an invented number.
  • Per-seat and per-user plans, which depend on team size rather than on data volume.
  • Request charges, retrieval fees on archive tiers, and early-deletion fees. These exist and can matter, but they depend on access patterns this calculator does not ask about.
  • Taxes, support plans and committed-use discounts.

How the numbers are built

Storage cost is the provider's own published per-GB or per-TB rate, scaled to the volume you entered. Where a provider publishes storage tiers, the tiers are applied cumulatively rather than as one flat rate — AWS S3 charges $0.023/GB for the first 50 TB and $0.021/GB above 500 TB, so billing a 1,000 TB deployment at the first-tier rate would overstate it by 6.7%. Egress cost applies that provider's actual policy — flat free, free up to a multiple of stored volume, a bundled allowance, volume tiers, or a flat per-TB rate. No rate here is estimated; each one is the number on the provider's pricing page or price list.

1 TB = 1,024 GB, matching the rest of this site. Where a provider bills per TB rather than per GB, its per-TB rate is used unchanged. Minimum commitments are applied: Wasabi's 1 TB storage floor and Storj's $5 monthly invoice both show up in the totals.
