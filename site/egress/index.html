The $92 download

Pulling 1 TB out of AWS S3 in a single month costs about $92 in egress alone — more than three times what it costs to store that terabyte for the same month. That asymmetry is the reason "egress-free" became a selling point, and it is the number every other provider in this table is implicitly competing against.

The same 1 TB is $0 at Cloudflare R2, Wasabi, Impossible Cloud, Filebase, UpCloud and Sevalla, and $7 at Storj. Storage price differences between these providers are measured in cents; the egress difference is measured in tens of dollars.

How this page is built

Egress policies are prose, not table cells, so they cannot be scraped the way prices are. Each row was read from the provider's own pricing page and carries the source link and a verification date. Last verified: 2026-10-22.

Cost-to-download figures use 1 TB = 1,024 GB. Where a provider bills per TB rather than per GB, its published per-TB rate is used unchanged. Free-allowance conditions are quoted as the provider states them, not paraphrased into a single number.

This page covers 30 providers. Where a provider publishes no egress term at all, the page says so instead of assuming zero — an absent clause is not a promise.

Frequently asked

  • Which cloud storage providers charge no egress fees?
    Of the object storage providers tracked here, Cloudflare R2, Filebase, Impossible Cloud, Sevalla, UpCloud, Wasabi bill nothing for egress. Each has different trade-offs elsewhere — minimum storage durations, minimum monthly charges, or a fair-use clause.
  • How much does it cost to download 1 TB from AWS S3?
    About $92.16. S3 bills $0.09/GB for the first 10 TB of internet egress per month in US East, dropping to $0.085, $0.07 and $0.05/GB at higher volumes. At 1,024 GB that first tier alone is $92.16.
  • What does free egress up to 3x storage actually mean?
    It means your monthly download allowance equals three times the volume you are storing, not three times your download. Backblaze B2 and IDrive e2 both work this way, so a 1 TB download is free once you store roughly 341 GB, and anything beyond three times your stored volume is billed per GB.
  • Do consumer cloud storage plans charge egress fees?
    No. The subscription products tracked here sell storage and seats, and transfer is part of the price with no separate egress line item. The exceptions publish a limit instead of a fee: OpenDrive caps its free plan at 1 GB/day, while Filen and Jottacloud advertise unlimited bandwidth on paid plans.
  • Is Cloudflare R2 really free to download from?
    Yes, for egress. R2 charges nothing for egress bandwidth on any storage class. Its Infrequent Access class does add a $0.01/GB retrieval fee, but that is a charge for reading stored data, not for sending it over the internet — a distinction worth checking before assuming any zero-egress claim is unconditional.
  • Why is egress so expensive at hyperscalers?
    Internet egress carries real transit and peering costs, and for providers that price storage below cost it is a margin line. The practical consequence is lock-in: data is cheap to put in and expensive to take out, which is exactly what zero-egress providers market against.
